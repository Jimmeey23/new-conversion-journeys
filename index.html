Three studios, three different funnels

2,907 new clients walked into the three studios in 2026 and 709 of them — 24% — bought something. Everything else in this report is a subdivision of that number.

The three are not running the same model. Kwality House acquires on free trial classes and full-price walk-ins. Supreme HQ runs the largest trial programme in the group and sells the Newcomers 2-for-1 as a follow-on purchase. Kenkere House fills its floor with hosted collaborations that barely convert, and does its real acquisition through the Intro Pack.

How a journey is counted

Every journey starts with the membership or class used on the first visit, taken from the day-end report — and where that field is blank, from the class or event name, which is how the hosted collaborations are identified.

An intro offer is only an intro offer when it is the thing that got the client in. If someone comes through a hosted class or a free trial and then buys a Newcomers 2-for-1 or an Intro Pack, that purchase is counted as their first purchase, as it should be. A full-price first visit is likewise a real first purchase and is mapped as one.

Conversion throughout means ever made a qualifying purchase. Retail and gift cards are excluded.

Hosted and collaboration classes are the largest source of new faces and convert at 3.9%. 879 people — 30% of all arrivals — came through run-club sessions, brand collabs and birthday takeovers, and 34 of them ever bought anything, for ₹4.0L in total.

The free trial class is the most productive route in the business. 810 arrivals, 31.1% conversion, ₹24,913 average spend per buyer and 29% ending up on an unlimited membership — better on every measure than either intro offer.

The Intro Pack converts better than the 2-for-1, at a third of the price. 37.8% against 25.5%. The 2-for-1 buyer who does convert spends more (₹19,798 against ₹11,792), but Bengaluru's cheaper pack gets far more people over the line.

Kwality House

Kemps Corner, Mumbai · 962 new clients · 271 bought · ₹47.3L

1,009 new clients walked in, 244 bought something, a median of 3 days later. 95 bought a second time, 47 ended up on an unlimited membership and 15 reached three months or longer. Average of 2.00 purchases and ₹18,060 of spend per buyer.

Kenkere House

Bengaluru · 936 new clients · 194 bought · ₹30.1L

Kenkere fills its floor with hosted collaborations — 395 of 936 arrivals, converting at 3% — and does its real acquisition through the New Client Intro Pack, which 209 people bought and which converts at 38%, the best route anywhere in the group.

Its flows are the most varied. The Intro Pack leads into a second intro offer (25), a single class (22), a 1-month unlimited (11) and an 8-class pack (10) — no single dominant next step. Buyers here make 2.27 purchases on average, 23% reach an unlimited membership and 10.8% upgrade to a longer commitment, all the best in the group.

The shape of it

936 new clients walked in, 194 bought something, a median of 2 days later. 89 bought a second time, 45 ended up on an unlimited membership and 12 reached three months or longer. Average of 2.27 purchases and ₹15,528 of spend per buyer.

What the numbers argue for

  • Decide what hosted classes are for. 879 arrivals, 34 buyers, ₹4.0L. If they are a brand and community play, stop counting them as acquisition. If they are acquisition, they need an offer made in the room on the day — the median converter from a hosted class takes 10 days, and the rest never come back.
  • Protect and expand the free trial class. It is the best route in the business on conversion, spend and membership rate all at once. Kenkere runs only 116 of them against Bandra's 371.
  • Copy the Intro Pack to Mumbai. At ₹709 it converts 37.8% against the 2-for-1's 25.5%, and its buyers reach memberships at the same rate.
  • Move the default first sale off the single class. Single-class first purchases are 37% of all buyers and produce ₹6,412 of spend against ₹23,721 for a 10–12 class pack and ₹39,259 for a 1-month unlimited.
  • Work the first month. Median gap to a second purchase is 15 days and the median upgrade lands within a month of the first purchase. Almost nothing changes after 90 days.

How this was built

Cohort

Every member in the day-end report flagged New - … whose first visit falls in 2026 at one of the three studios — 2,907 people (962 / 1,009 / 936). Repeat visits, staff and family classes and records with no visit type are excluded, which is why these totals match the studio's own new-client counts to within a record or two.

Arrival route

Taken from the membership or class used on the first visit. Where that field is blank (939 records), the class or event name decides it: anything reading as a collaboration, hosted class, run club or birthday takeover is a hosted class, and the rest fall to whether a payment exists around the visit.

Purchases

Every service purchase in order, retail and gift cards excluded. A Newcomers 2-for-1 or New Client Intro Pack counts as a purchase unless it is the transaction that got the client in — identified as the earliest intro purchase, plus any duplicate of it within two days. A full-price first visit is counted as the first purchase.

Two conversion numbers

The studio dashboard's Converted flag counts a purchase made after the trial visit and treats the Intro Pack as a purchase: 226 / 193 / 161. This report counts anyone who ever made a qualifying purchase: 271 / 244 / 194.

Caveats

2026 is a part year ending 16 September 2026, so August and September arrivals have had little time to come back. 42 clients attended on a paid product with no matching transaction in the sales file — guest passes, corporate or family-paid classes — and are counted as non-buyers. Around a quarter of second purchases land on the same day as the first, so repeat is reported both ways. Monthly-billed memberships appear as repeat purchases.

Not included

The Pop-up line, which is a different business — Copper + Cloves credits and single classes at an average ticket under ₹900, with effectively no memberships.
